# Public expert data and teacher policies for our traffic study

**Follow-up completed:** the team generated compatible local teacher advice and
tested it in [pedestrian fine-tuning](../results/pedestrian-finetune-cluster-review/REVIEW.md).
No external expert dataset was silently substituted. The review below records
the decision that preceded that experiment.

Reviewed 3 November 2026 using author repositories, research papers and official
SUMO documentation. This review does not import an external controller, load
external checkpoints, download a bulk dataset or launch new training.

## Recommendation

Public demonstrations exist, but no inspected resource is a verified drop-in
pedestrian teacher for our observation schema, three service actions, safe
transition machine and crossing-level p95 target. The most direct experiment is
to implement/adapt a documented teacher in our existing environment, validate
its actual performance, and collect compatible demonstrations. Keep the
vehicle-improving MAPPO checkpoints as the fine-tuning starting points and
compare against continued PPO without imitation.

Expert is a relative performance description, not proof of optimality. A
teacher good at vehicle queues may be poor at pedestrian tails. Even a strong
teacher can trade vehicle service for pedestrian delay.

## What the public sources provide

### RESCO, not the unverified name RESUME

The verified project is [RESCO](https://github.com/Pi-Star-Lab/RESCO), a traffic
signal benchmark with scenario/demand files and controller implementations,
including Fixed Time, Max Pressure and Max Wave. I did not identify the
"RESUME" benchmark described in the supplied text. RESCO is useful for external
benchmarks and teacher methods; its advertised network/demand resources are not
automatically expert state-action demonstrations, and no directly compatible
pedestrian-demonstration dataset was verified here.

### LibSignal: controller library

[LibSignal](https://github.com/DaRL-LibSignal/LibSignal) supplies cross-simulator
infrastructure. Its [agent directory](https://github.com/DaRL-LibSignal/LibSignal/tree/master/agent)
includes fixed-time, MaxPressure and SOTL implementations. The inspected
[MaxPressure agent](https://github.com/DaRL-LibSignal/LibSignal/blob/master/agent/maxpressure.py)
uses vehicle-lane counts and phase pressures; it is not a verified pedestrian
tail-wait teacher. Webster was not present in the inspected agent list, so do
not repeat the claim that this library necessarily supplies it. Installing the
whole Docker/dependency stack is unnecessary for understanding these methods.

### DataLight: actual downloadable offline interaction data

The author [dataset guide](https://github.com/LiangZhang1996/DataLight/blob/main/memory/readme.md)
describes expert trajectories generated by Advanced-CoLight, medium trajectories
from FRAP, and random/fixed-time data, with action intervals of 10, 15 or 20
seconds. A [Zenodo release](https://zenodo.org/records/20822844) contains two ZIP
files totalling 412.4 MB compressed. The guide also links a Google Drive folder.
The archive size after extraction was not verified; bulk files were not fetched.

The [public configuration](https://github.com/LiangZhang1996/DataLight/blob/main/utils/config.py)
uses vehicle-lane features and four vehicle phases. The
[paper's state/action definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.10828v2#S4.SS1)
and CityFlow experiments do not supply our pedestrian state or protected PED
action. This is a useful offline-learning resource, but cannot directly teach
our model when an individual crossing needs service. A new mapping/transfer
experiment would be needed; absent pedestrian fields must not be invented or
filled with zeros and treated as observed measurements. The author's repository
has changed publication titles/version metadata; cite the specific paper and
dataset version actually used.

### DemoLight: strong methodological precedent, data availability unverified

[Learning Traffic Signal Control from Demonstrations](https://jhc.sjtu.edu.cn/~gjzheng/paper/cikm2019_demolight/cikm2019_demolight_paper.pdf)
uses conventional-controller demonstrations to initialise actor and critic,
followed by reinforcement learning. It supports studying demonstration-assisted
traffic learning, but does not establish our every-crossing fairness target.
The [author code](https://github.com/xyh97/DemoLight) contains an expert-data
loader. Its [configuration](https://github.com/xyh97/DemoLight/blob/master/Config.py)
references `../frap/demo.p`; this review did not verify a standalone downloadable
pedestrian demonstration file. Do not describe the repository's traffic inputs
as if they were such a file.

### LemgoRL: pedestrian-inclusive benchmark with an important dependency

[LemgoRL](https://github.com/RL-INA/LemgoRL) includes a real-junction SUMO scenario,
vehicle and pedestrian demand modelling, a greedy policy and documented PPO
checkpoint use. Its traffic-logic setup requires the proprietary Lisa+
OmlFgServer, which the repository cannot distribute. It is relevant to scenario
design and traffic engineering; the documented full setup is not a completely
free, ready-to-run teacher for this project. No compatible exported demonstration
dataset was verified.

### Pedestrian-and-vehicle DQN at a Washington, DC junction

The [GW/Uzilina author repository](https://github.com/wissamsleiman/Deep-Reinforcement-Learning-for-Traffic-Signal-Control)
provides a Foggy Bottom SUMO scenario, traffic/signal data and documented trained
models. Its [associated paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03611981251384965)
studies vehicle/pedestrian connectivity. This is a relevant external candidate
to investigate, not a demonstrated superior teacher for our network. The README
also describes OMNeT++ co-simulation and additional simulation assets; those
dependencies have not been installed or tested here.

The [test settings](https://github.com/wissamsleiman/Deep-Reinforcement-Learning-for-Traffic-Signal-Control/blob/Master/DRL_Control/testing_settings.ini)
declare 27 state inputs and three actions. Matching action counts do not imply
matching meanings. The [testing code](https://github.com/wissamsleiman/Deep-Reinforcement-Learning-for-Traffic-Signal-Control/blob/Master/DRL_Control/testing_simulation.py)
uses junction-specific signal indices and SUMO person waitingTime, unlike our
crossing-arrival-to-entry events. Inspect movement mappings, observation
semantics and clearance before attempting any transfer. Downloaded weights have
not been loaded or evaluated.

### Helsinki pedestrian-aware MAPPO: author request

The [curiosity-enhanced MAPPO paper](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/itr2.70247)
states that supporting data are available from the corresponding author on
reasonable request. This review did not verify a public demonstration or
checkpoint download. A useful request would seek the network, pedestrian demand,
phase/action definitions, reward definitions, checkpoints and any evaluation
traces. An article PDF alone is not reusable training data. No author has been
contacted by this review.

## Corrections to the proposed SUMO data-collection recipe

SUMO supports gap-based actuation, NEMA and dynamic phase selection with
pedestrian priority. Their quality depends on configuration and demand; the
controller type does not automatically make it an optimised expert.
[Official traffic-light documentation](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Traffic_Lights.html).

Webster timing adaptation is available through
[`tlsCycleAdaptation.py`](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Tools/tls.html#tlscycleadaptationpy),
which computes cycle/green timing from demand. It is not itself a generic
pedestrian-aware online teacher. Adapting admission/clearance durations freely
would change our primary action/safety comparison. Use compatible timing plans
or label a broader-action experiment separately.

Logging the active SUMO phase is not sufficient as an imitation target. In our
system, a request for pedestrian service can first produce vehicle yellow, then
all-red, then pedestrian green. Record the *requested service action* at genuine
decision opportunities and distinguish it from the executed phase. Preserve the
legal-action mask, phase age, controller state, local traffic/pedestrian features,
scenario, seed, and source identity. Forced clearance ticks must not overwhelm
the actor's supervised examples. SUMO's
[TraCI retrieval API](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/TraCI/Traffic_Lights_Value_Retrieval.html)
provides signal state/program data; reconstructing an internal controller's
intent still requires care.

Our existing teacher-data pipeline already generates demonstrations from our
actuated baseline. Its local action transcription is checked against actual
baseline decisions. The next improvement should test a stronger teacher or
different fine-tuning sequence, not assume that an external filename makes the
data higher quality.

## Concrete next experiment

1. Complete diagnosis of crossing lockouts before interpreting congested policy
   performance. A teacher cannot act while the common safety machine has no
   available action. Correct any physical simulation defect for every controller.
2. Implement a small set of candidate teacher rules using our legal phase
   requests: current actuated; documented gap/queue-responsive control with
   explicit pedestrian priority; and a pressure-inspired rule with pedestrian
   urgency. A modified rule is an adaptation, not an exact published controller.
3. Tune only on training/validation demand. Compare the teachers directly on
   vehicle service/loss, every crossing's p95, unserved requests and completion.
   Keep an external method's name separate from its measured performance here.
4. Collect teacher labels on both teacher rollouts and the states actually visited
   by each vehicle-improving MAPPO replica. Use no hidden future demand, and keep
   teacher information local if it is to label decentralised actor decisions.
5. Compare continued original-objective MAPPO, pedestrian-focused fine-tuning,
   and the same pedestrian-focused fine-tuning plus guidance. Use identical
   added budgets, all starting replicas and fresh matched evaluation seeds.
   Keep the frozen original models, actual teachers and conventional baselines
   in evaluation; assess both imitation fidelity and traffic outcomes.

External data may also support a separate transfer-learning study, but would add
representation/action mapping and simulator-distribution questions. It should
not silently replace the original synthetic experiment. Before using external
code or data, pin the version, retain attribution and check the applicable code
and dataset terms separately. No earlier CS3263 artifacts are needed.

With the storage constraint, retain this compact source review and inspect
metadata/small samples first. Prefer generating compact, compatible local
demonstrations over downloading whole frameworks or the 412.4 MB DataLight
release without a demonstrated mapping to the pedestrian task.
