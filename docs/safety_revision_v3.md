# Safety revision before the final test

The first locked-seed evaluation attempted 13 scenarios on seeds 7001–7010. It completed 120 scenario–seed groups, but **all ten 80-person pedestrian-burst groups failed a runtime safety assertion**: a person appeared on a C1 crossing while the controller displayed vehicle green. No complete v2 summary was produced. These runs are a safety diagnostic, not final performance evidence.

The 80 additional people departed over 41 seconds toward one route. SUMO reported pedestrians jammed on crossing `:C1_c3`; in a diagnostic replay, one later entered `:C1_c0` after pedestrian clearance had ended. [SUMO's pedestrian documentation](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.html#jamming) says its striping model can move jammed people through obstacles after a threshold. The observed safety failure is enough to reject this stress definition; the documentation does not by itself establish every step of its cause.

The revised common controller requires **three consecutive observations without a person on any crossing** before leaving pedestrian clearance, in addition to the original clearance duration. A trial that instead held all red whenever anyone occupied an internal walking area caused gridlock and was discarded. The concentrated burst was reduced to **20 additional people over 41 seconds** at one crossing. It remains a local burst relative to the 120 background pedestrians spread among the network's routes. A development pilot on seeds 7001–7003 ran all 33 controller/replica combinations per seed on this revised burst, with no safety assertion or SUMO pedestrian-jam warning. These are safety checks, not performance tuning or final results.

The development safety pilot was then extended to **all ten previously inspected seeds 7001–7010**: 330 matched controller/replica runs completed, with no safety assertion and no pedestrian-jam warning. This validates the revised stress case on development seeds only; it is not a guarantee for new seeds.

Before accepting `runs/evaluation-v3/` as final, a further protocol issue was identified: its policies had been trained under the earlier clearance rule, whereas its baselines and policies were evaluated under the three-empty-step rule. A single training-episode replay matched exactly, but that was insufficient to establish equivalence for 150 episodes and five replicas. The partial 8001–8010 evaluation was stopped; its groups are diagnostic only.

All 30 IPPO/MAPPO replicas are therefore retrained from their specified initialisation seeds under the revised safety rule in `models/v3/` and `runs/training-v3/`. The **same validation-only extension rule** is reapplied to those new learning curves before any final test result is inspected. The final evaluation uses **new untouched demand seeds 9001–9010**, all 13 scenarios, and a separate `runs/evaluation-v4/` directory if the rule selects 150 episodes. Seeds 7001–7010 and 8001–8010 are marked exploratory in `config/evaluation.json`; older checkpoints remain separate. The final evaluator records revised source, configuration, model, demand and SUMO-version provenance. If a final safety assertion occurs, its affected group is invalid and must be investigated before any performance claim.
